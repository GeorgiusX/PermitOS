## 1. Company Overview

### What Is PermitOS?

PermitOS is an AI-native permit compliance firm operating in the Florida real estate and construction industry. It is not a generic SaaS tool and it is not a marketplace. It is an operating model that delivers permit compliance as a managed service — with licensed professionals in the loop and AI performing the core analysis work underneath.

The platform is built around a single, high-value problem: architectural and engineering plan sets are regularly submitted to building departments with errors, omissions, and code conflicts that cause costly rejections and project delays. PermitOS catches these issues before submission — not after. This positions PermitOS upstream of every other tool in the permitting workflow.

### Mission

To eliminate preventable permit rejections in Florida real estate by building the most intelligent, domain-specific plan review system in the market — one that gets smarter with every project submitted through the platform.

### Positioning Statement

PermitOS is described as an "AI-native permit compliance firm." This framing is precise:

- **AI-native** — AI is not a feature layered onto an existing workflow; it is the core of how compliance review work is performed.
- **Permit compliance** — the product output is a code compliance verdict on a plan set, not a submission tracker or form-fill tool.
- **Firm** — PermitOS delivers outcomes, not software licenses. Licensed professionals (architects, private plan review providers, expeditors) are affiliated with the platform and review AI output before it reaches a client or city.

## 2. The Problem

Permit rejection is one of the most expensive and underappreciated pain points in real estate development. When a building department rejects a permit application, the project stalls. Carrying costs accumulate. Architects, engineers, and expeditors must rework documents, resubmit, and wait again — a cycle that can cost weeks or months per project.

The root cause of most rejections is fragmentation across the production and review chain:

- Architectural drawings, MEP (mechanical, electrical, plumbing) engineering, and structural engineering are produced by separate subcontractors who rarely cross-check each other's work before a package goes forward.
- Local regulatory requirements — Florida Building Code, Miami-Dade regulations, Miami 21 zoning, Miami Beach municipal codes — are complex, hyper-local, and frequently updated.
- Private plan review providers, who perform pre-submission compliance review under Florida Statute §553.791, currently rely on manual review processes with no AI assistance.
- There is no automated system that checks a complete plan set against specific municipal requirements before submission.
- Current permitting software manages the submission process but does not validate the content of the plans.

> *The gap PermitOS fills: existing tools tell you how to submit. PermitOS tells you whether you will pass.*

## 3. The Florida Permit Workflow

Understanding the full workflow is essential to understanding where PermitOS fits and who the platform serves. The Florida permitting process involves five distinct parties, each with a specific role:

| Step | Party | Role | Pain Point |
|------|-------|------|------------|
| 1 | Architect / MEP Engineer | Produces architectural drawings, MEP plans, and supporting documentation that form the permit package. | Drawings may contain code conflicts or omissions not caught until city review causes rejection. |
| 2 | Private Plan Review Provider | Licensed firm (under FL §553.791) that independently reviews the plan set for code compliance, requests corrections from the architect, and approves the corrected package before submission. | Review is manual, time-consuming, and dependent on individual expertise. No AI tooling exists to assist. |
| 3 | Permit Expeditor | Receives the reviewed and approved package from the private provider and handles submission to the municipal building department, tracks status, and manages city communications. | Even approved packages can contain issues; expeditor must manage back-and-forth with city. |
| 4 | Municipal Building Department | Reviews submitted package, issues permits, or returns RFIs (Requests for Information) and rejections. | Overloaded departments create multi-week review cycles. Private provider approval must legally be accepted under FL §553.791. |
| 5 | Real Estate Developer | The financial stakeholder funding the project. Does not interact with the permit system directly but absorbs all delay costs. | Every week of delay is a carrying cost. No visibility into permit status without manual updates from architect or expeditor. |

PermitOS sits primarily at **Steps 2 and 3** — powering the private provider's review process with AI and giving the expeditor a validated, de-risked package to submit. It also provides the architect (Step 1) with early-stage compliance feedback during plan production, and the developer (Step 5) with real-time project visibility.

## 4. Who We Serve

### Primary Users — Private Plan Review Providers

Private providers operating under Florida Statute §553.791 are the platform's most strategic user segment. These are licensed architects, engineers, or building code administrator firms hired by developers or contractors to perform an independent code-compliance review of plan sets before submission to the city. When a private provider approves a package, the municipal building department is legally required to accept it.

- **Why they are the primary user:** the private provider's entire value proposition is the quality and speed of their plan review. PermitOS is the AI layer that makes that review faster, more thorough, and more consistent.
- **Why they pay:** faster review cycles mean more projects handled per month; higher accuracy means fewer rejections and a stronger professional reputation.
- **PermitOS's role:** the AI Compliance Report is the private provider's AI-assisted review output. The provider reviews, edits, and signs off on the report — maintaining their legal responsibility while operating at significantly higher efficiency.
- This mirrors the **attorney-in-the-loop model**: AI drafts the review; the licensed professional approves it.

### Secondary Users — Architects & MEP Engineers

Architects and MEP engineering firms produce the plan sets that enter the review chain. They are the source of the documents and bear reputational and financial risk when their work generates rejections. Smaller practices (without in-house compliance staff) are the ideal entry point.

- **Why they pay:** a rejection delays their client's project and damages their professional standing. Pre-submission AI review is a professional insurance policy.
- **Willingness-to-pay signal:** estimated $200–$500 per submission validation, directly tied to avoided rework costs.
- MEP engineers are natural adjacent users — they are already part of the same permit package workflow.

### Tertiary Users — Permit Expeditors

Permit expeditors manage high submission volumes — typically 20–50 active projects simultaneously across multiple municipalities. They receive packages from private providers (or directly from architects when no private provider is engaged) and handle all city-facing logistics.

- **Why they pay:** a rejection is a direct reputational hit with their developer clients. Receiving a pre-validated package reduces their risk and speeds their workflow.
- **Positioning risk:** expeditors must be positioned as beneficiaries of PermitOS, not targets of replacement. The platform makes them more efficient, not redundant.

### Indirect Clients — Real Estate Developers

Developers are the financial stakeholders behind every project. They do not use the platform directly but absorb every delay as a carrying cost. Developers with established relationships with architects and expeditors can drive top-down adoption by requiring their project teams to use PermitOS.

| User Segment | Role in Workflow | Primary Value | Monetization |
|---|---|---|---|
| Private Plan Review Provider | Reviews & approves plans (FL §553.791) | AI-powered review: faster, more accurate, scalable | Subscription or per-review fee |
| Architect / MEP Engineer | Produces plan set | Pre-submission compliance check; fewer rejections | Per-submission or subscription |
| Permit Expeditor | Submits to city, manages tracking | Receives validated packages; lower rejection risk | Per-submission or subscription |
| Real Estate Developer | Project funder; indirect client | Faster approvals; real-time project visibility | Phase 2: project-level access |

## 5. The Platform

PermitOS is a vertically integrated, domain-specific platform for permit compliance. It is not a general project management tool. Every feature is designed around the specific stages, parties, and documents involved in a Florida real estate permit submission. The platform has three structural layers, each unlocked in sequence as the product matures.

### Layer 1 — AI Compliance Engine

The core product. Ingests architectural and engineering plan sets (PDF, CAD, image formats) and checks them against the relevant regulatory framework for the target municipality. Outputs a structured AI Compliance Report.

**Regulatory frameworks covered (expanding over time):**
- Florida Building Code (FBC)
- Miami-Dade County regulations
- Miami 21 zoning code
- Miami Beach municipal codes and design standards
- DERM tree preservation rules (Miami-Dade)
- Cross-discipline conflict detection (architectural vs. MEP vs. structural)

**AI Compliance Report contents:**
- Overall risk score for the submission
- Specific code violations with regulatory section references
- Flagged elements with plan page and location citations
- Recommended correction per issue
- Evidence confidence scoring (strong / medium / weak) highlighting where human attention is most needed

No AI output is delivered to a client, expeditor, or city without licensed professional review. The private plan review provider reviews, edits, and signs off on the compliance report — maintaining full legal accountability while operating with AI efficiency.

### Layer 2 — Municipality Intelligence Database

A proprietary, maintained database of regulatory requirements across Florida municipalities. This is PermitOS's primary long-term competitive moat. Florida permit requirements are hyper-local — every city and county has its own codes, checklists, fee structures, and review preferences. The AI queries this database per submission to ensure every compliance check is jurisdiction-specific.

- **Launch coverage:** Miami Beach, Miami-Dade County, North Bay Village.
- **Expansion:** City of Miami, Coral Gables, and additional Miami-Dade municipalities in Phase 2–3.
- Maintained through direct engagement with building departments, licensed partner relationships, and ongoing regulatory monitoring.
- No competitor can replicate this quickly; it requires real submissions, professional relationships, and time to accumulate.

### Layer 3 — Project Workspace & Collaboration

The workflow layer connecting all parties on a project. Unlike simpler two-party tools, PermitOS must support up to five parties on a single project — architect, MEP engineer, private provider, expeditor, and developer — each with different access levels, responsibilities, and views. The workspace is built on role-based access, not a single unified dashboard.

### Feature Breakdown

#### 1. AI Compliance Report
The primary platform deliverable. Generated after a plan set is uploaded and analyzed by the AI engine. Structured as a professional document — not a chat response — with violation flags, code references, confidence scoring, and recommended corrections. Formatted for architect-to-private-provider and provider-to-expeditor handoff.

#### 2. Submission Completeness Checklist
Before AI compliance review runs, the platform validates that the package contains everything the target municipality requires: correct document types, required application forms, supporting materials, and fee calculations. This is an administrative pre-flight check, separate from code compliance analysis.

#### 3. Document Management with Review-Cycle Version Control
Plan sets go through multiple revision cycles between architect, private provider, and expeditor. Each document version is linked to the compliance report generated against it. When a corrected version is uploaded, the system tracks whether flagged issues from the prior review have been resolved — creating a complete revision history tied to compliance outcomes.

#### 4. Multi-Party Role-Based Access
Each project has configurable roles with different permissions. A developer sees project status and approval timeline only. A permit expeditor sees the full compliance report and submission checklist. An MEP engineer sees only the sections of the review relevant to their discipline. A private provider sees everything and can annotate the AI report before approving it. No single view is forced on all parties.

#### 5. Smart Task Management
Each permit project is broken into stages mapped to the actual Florida permit workflow: plan upload → AI compliance review → private provider review → architect revision → provider approval → expeditor submission → city review → approval or RFI response. Every task shows who is responsible, what is required, and when it is due.

#### 6. Automated Progress Notifications
Structured notifications trigger at each workflow milestone: AI review complete, issues flagged, revision required, provider approved, package submitted, city RFI received, permit issued. Notifications are specific and actionable — not generic status pings — so each party knows exactly what is needed from them.

#### 7. Activity Log & Audit Trail
Every action is logged and timestamped: document uploads, AI review runs, compliance report versions, provider annotations, user comments, revision submissions, city responses. The log is immutable and functions as a liability-protection record for all parties and a quality audit trail for the platform.

#### 8. RFI & Rejection Response Tracking
When a city building department issues a Request for Information or rejection, it enters the platform as a structured event: what was flagged, which discipline owns the response, what revision was submitted, and whether the city accepted it. This closes the loop on the most painful part of the permit cycle — the back-and-forth that currently lives in email.

#### 9. In-Portal Communications
All inter-party communication is threaded and linked to specific documents, tasks, compliance flags, or city comments. No email. Every message has context. Communication history is preserved with the project record across the full lifecycle, eliminating the need to re-explain context as a project moves between parties.

#### 10. Hard Deadline Tracking
Permits expire. Extension deadlines are absolute. Inspection windows open and close. PermitOS surfaces these deadlines as first-class alerts — not buried in an activity log — because missed deadlines in construction permitting can void months of approved work.

## 7. Go-To-Market Strategy

### Beachhead Market: Miami Beach

Miami Beach is the primary launch market. It combines a high density of real estate development activity, a complex local regulatory environment (Miami Beach municipal codes plus Miami-Dade overlays), and an established community of architects, private providers, and expeditors who work the same projects repeatedly. Depth in Miami Beach before breadth across Miami-Dade.

### Phase 1 — Supply Side (Months 1–6)

**Goal:** 10–20 active architects, private providers, and/or expeditors on the platform.

- Use co-founder / partner network to access private review providers and expeditors already active in Miami Beach.
- Offer free or discounted access in exchange for structured feedback and documented submissions.
- Document the first successful permit submission powered by PermitOS as a proof case.
- Focus: landscape compliance first, then expand to full permit packages within the same municipality.

### Phase 2 — Demand Side (Months 4–8)

**Goal:** first developers on the platform as reference clients.

- Partner with developer co-founder / investor as the first reference client.
- Activate top-down adoption pressure: developer requires their architects, private providers, and expeditors to use PermitOS on shared projects.
- Expand municipality coverage to City of Miami and wider Miami-Dade.

### Phase 3 — Scale (Months 8–18)

- Full Miami-Dade municipal coverage with rejection pattern intelligence.
- White-label offering for enterprise developer or GC accounts.
- Begin South Florida expansion beyond Miami-Dade (Broward County, Palm Beach County).

## 9. Competitive Landscape

### Primary Reference: PermitFlow

PermitFlow is the best-funded platform in the permitting space, having raised $54 million in Series B funding led by Accel with participation from Kleiner Perkins and Y Combinator. It is the category leader in permitting workflow automation. However, PermitFlow and PermitOS solve different problems at different points in the workflow:

| | PermitFlow | PermitOS |
|---|---|---|
| Core question answered | How do I submit this permit correctly? | Will my plan set pass code review? |
| What the AI analyzes | Municipal requirements & application forms | Architectural and engineering plan sets |
| When it intervenes | During and after submission | Before submission — during review |
| Primary user | General contractors, developers | Private providers, architects, expeditors |
| Business model | SaaS subscription | AI-native firm (outcomes-based) |
| Geographic scope | Nationwide breadth | Florida depth first (Miami Beach) |
| Plan set code validation | No | Yes — core product |
| Private provider support | No | Yes — primary user segment |

> *PermitFlow and PermitOS are complementary, not identical. An architect could use PermitOS to validate plan compliance and then use PermitFlow to manage submission logistics. The two tools sit at different points in the same pipeline.*

### Other Players

- **Pulley:** permit platform focused on submission tracking and fast permit outcomes. Similar positioning to PermitFlow, not focused on plan set compliance.
- **GreenLite:** AI-powered plan review tool primarily serving municipalities — the government side of review, not private sector professionals.
- **CivCheck:** operates in the permit compliance space with limited Florida-specific depth or public product detail.
- **Procore / Autodesk Construction Cloud:** broad construction project management platforms with no permit-specific AI compliance review.

### Competitive Moat

PermitOS's defensibility compounds over time through four mechanisms:

1. **Municipality Intelligence Database:** built from real submissions and direct regulatory relationships. Cannot be replicated quickly by a competitor entering the market.
2. **Rejection Pattern Database:** as submissions accumulate, the platform builds a proprietary dataset of what gets rejected in each municipality and why — enabling predictive risk scoring no competitor can match without real Florida data.
3. **Private Provider Network:** onboarded private providers create switching costs and generate the workflow data that continuously improves AI accuracy.
4. **First-mover depth in Miami Beach:** local regulatory expertise, building department relationships, and professional community trust are hard to acquire at scale from outside the market.
